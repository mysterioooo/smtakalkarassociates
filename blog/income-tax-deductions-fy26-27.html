Key Income Tax Deductions You Might Be Missing in FY 26-27

Learn how smart deduction planning under Sections 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D, 80E and 80G can reduce your tax liability in the new financial year.

As we navigate through the financial year 2026-27, tax planning remains a critical exercise for salaried professionals, independent consultants, and business owners alike. While standard deductions under Section 80C are widely known, there are several nuances and additional sections that taxpayers often overlook.

Whether you have opted for the Old Tax Regime to claim exemptions or are assessing if the New Tax Regime is beneficial, maximizing your eligible deductions before March 31st can significantly reduce your tax liability.

1 Beyond the 1.5 Lakh Limit: Section 80CCD(1B)

Many taxpayers exhaust their ₹1.5 Lakh limit under Section 80C through EPF, PPF, Life Insurance premiums, and ELSS. However, you can claim an additional deduction of ₹50,000 by investing in the National Pension System (NPS) under Section 80CCD(1B). This brings your total potential deduction for investments to ₹2 Lakhs.

2 Preventive Health Check-ups (Section 80D)

While most people claim the premium paid for health insurance under Section 80D, they often forget about the preventive health check-up allowance. You can claim up to ₹5,000 for preventive health check-ups for yourself, your spouse, dependent children, or parents. Note that this ₹5,000 is subsumed within the overall limit of ₹25,000 (or ₹50,000 for senior citizens) of Section 80D.

"Effective tax planning shouldn't start in March. A systematic approach throughout the year ensures better cash flow and optimized returns without the last-minute scramble."

3 Education Loan Interest (Section 80E)

If you have taken an education loan for yourself, your spouse, or your children, the interest component of the EMI is fully deductible under Section 80E.

  • There is no upper limit on the amount of interest you can claim.
  • The deduction is available for a maximum of 8 years starting from the year you begin repaying the interest.
  • This is only applicable if the loan is taken from a recognized financial institution or approved charitable organization.

4 Donations to Charitable Funds (Section 80G)

Contributions made to certain relief funds and charitable institutions can be claimed as a deduction under Section 80G. Depending on the institution, you can claim either 100% or 50% of the donated amount, with or without restriction. Always ensure you receive a valid receipt with the organization's PAN and registration number.

Choosing the Right Tax Regime

It is important to remember that most of the deductions mentioned above (like 80C, 80D, and 80E) are only available under the Old Tax Regime. If you opt for the New Tax Regime, you forfeit these deductions in exchange for lower slab rates.
